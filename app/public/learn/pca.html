01 — Mathematical Foundations

PCA: The House of Knowledge

Master the linear algebra behind Principal Component Analysis — from mean centering and covariance matrices to eigen-decomposition and explained variance ratios.

💡

Principal Component Analysis for Beginners (Explain Like I'm 5)

Imagine taking a 2D photo of a 3D object like a teapot. If you take a photo from the wrong angle, it looks like a flat blob and loses all detail. If you tilt your camera to capture its longest diagonal (from handle to spout), you capture maximum detail in 2D! PCA finds the single best camera angle (PC1) that preserves maximum spread (variance) while compressing 100 features down to 2!

🎯 1. Center the Data

Shift all points so the middle of the data cloud sits at the origin $(0, 0)$.

↗️ 2. Find PC1 (Max Spread)

Draw an axis line through the cloud in the direction where points spread out the furthest (maximum variance).

📐 3. Find PC2 (90° Angle)

Draw a second axis strictly perpendicular (90° right angle) to PC1 to capture the remaining variation without overlap!

🎯

1. Mean Centering & Standardizing

Prior to computing variance, input dataset \(\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times P}\) must be zero-centered to eliminate origin translation bias:

$$\mathbf{X}_{\text{centered}} = \mathbf{X} - \boldsymbol{\mu}$$
📐

2. Sample Covariance Matrix

Measures linear pairwise feature alignment across all $P$ dimensions:

$$\mathbf{\Sigma} = \frac{1}{N - 1} \mathbf{X}_{\text{centered}}^T \mathbf{X}_{\text{centered}}$$
⚡

3. Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors

Solves characteristic linear matrix system to identify orthogonal direction vectors \(\mathbf{v}_i\) and variance magnitude scalars \(\lambda_i\):

$$\mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{v}_i = \lambda_i \mathbf{v}_i$$
📊

4. Explained Variance Ratio

The fraction of total dataset variance preserved by the top $k$ principal component vectors:

$$\text{Ratio}_k = \frac{\lambda_k}{\sum_{j=1}^P \lambda_j}$$

💡 Interview & Production Guidelines

Q: Why is feature scaling mandatory before PCA?

Features measured in larger numeric units naturally exhibit higher raw variance. Without standardization, PCA would falsely assign top principal components to large-scale features regardless of true signal variance.

Q: PCA vs. Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)?

SVD computes principal components directly on feature matrix $\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{U \Sigma V}^T$ without explicitly forming the $\mathcal{O}(P^2)$ covariance matrix, offering higher numerical precision and stability.

Q: What are the main limitations of PCA?

PCA assumes linear relationships between features. For non-linear manifold structures (e.g. spirals or concentric rings), non-linear reduction methods like Kernel PCA, t-SNE, or UMAP perform significantly better.

Q: Computational Complexity Summary

Covariance Matrix: $\mathcal{O}(N P^2)$.
Eigen-decomposition: $\mathcal{O}(P^3)$.
Projection: $\mathcal{O}(N P k)$ operations.
