01 — Mathematical Foundations

Hierarchical Clustering: The House of Knowledge

Explore bottom-up agglomerative merging, dendrogram construction, and linkage distance formulas.

💡

Hierarchical Clustering for Beginners (Explain Like I'm 5)

Imagine building a Family Tree (Dendrogram) from scratch. At first, every single person is their own independent leaf. First, you pair up identical twins who live closest together. Then you merge siblings into families, and finally merge families into giant clans! Hierarchical Clustering builds a full bottom-up tree hierarchy where you can cut the branches at any height to get your desired number of clusters!

🌱 1. Start with Singletons

Every single data point starts as its own mini cluster of size 1 ($N$ total clusters).

🤝 2. Merge Closest Pair

Find the two closest clusters in the dataset according to a linkage rule (e.g. Ward / Single) and fuse them into one!

✂️ 3. Slice the Dendrogram

Draw a horizontal line across the tree hierarchy at distance threshold $h$ to cut the tree into $K$ final clusters.

🔗

1. Single Linkage (Minimum Distance)

Measures the smallest pairwise distance between any single sample in cluster $A$ and any sample in cluster $B$:

$$D_{\text{single}}(A, B) = \min_{x \in A, y \in B} d(x, y)$$
📏

2. Complete Linkage (Maximum Distance)

Measures the largest pairwise distance between cluster elements, forming compact spherical clusters:

$$D_{\text{complete}}(A, B) = \max_{x \in A, y \in B} d(x, y)$$
⚖️

3. Average Linkage (UPGMA)

Averages all pairwise distances between items in cluster $A$ and cluster $B$:

$$D_{\text{avg}}(A, B) = \frac{1}{|A||B|} \sum_{x \in A} \sum_{y \in B} d(x, y)$$
📊

4. Ward's Minimum Variance

Minimizes the total within-cluster sum of squared error increase (\(\Delta \text{ESS}\)) upon merging:

$$\Delta \text{ESS} = \frac{|A||B|}{|A| + |B|} \|\boldsymbol{\mu}_A - \boldsymbol{\mu}_B\|^2$$

💡 Interview & Production Guidelines

Q: Why is Hierarchical Clustering deterministic?

Unlike K-Means (which depends on random centroid initialization), Agglomerative Clustering produces the exact same merge tree hierarchy on every execution given fixed distance metrics and linkage criteria.

Q: How does a Dendrogram determine cluster count K?

Drawing a horizontal cut line across a Dendrogram tree at distance threshold $h$ severs tree branches, directly defining the final number of flat clusters $K$.

Q: What is the main drawback of Agglomerative Clustering?

High computational complexity: $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ naive or $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ optimized runtime. It becomes slow for large datasets ($N > 50,000$).

Q: Computational Complexity Summary

Distance Matrix: $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ storage space.
Merge Tree Building: $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ operations.
Dendrogram Cut: $\mathcal{O}(N)$ lookup time.

📊 Algorithmic Comparison: Hierarchical vs K-Means vs DBSCAN

Algorithm Cluster Shape Requires K in advance? Outlier / Noise Handling Best Used When
Hierarchical Nested Tree Hierarchy No (Cut tree at threshold) Sensitive (Leaves as singletons) Taxonomy, gene expression & hierarchical structure discovery.
K-Means Spherical / Convex Yes (Must specify K) Poor (Pulls centroids toward noise) Large-scale flat clustering with known $K$.
DBSCAN Arbitrary / Non-convex No (Density $\varepsilon$, MinPts) Excellent (Flags noise explicitly) Spatial GPS clusters & irregular shapes with noise.
